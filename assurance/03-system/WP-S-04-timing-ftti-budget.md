## 1. Purpose

Fault tolerant time intervals and the detection and reaction budget along the command path, including the fallback. The fault tolerant time interval (FTTI) of a safety goal is how long a fault may persist before the goal can be violated (ISO 26262-1). Each safety mechanism of [WP-S-03](WP-S-03-technical-safety-concept-architecture.md) must detect the faults it covers and bring the item to a safe state within it.

Needed at gate G2 ([WP-M-00](../01-management/WP-M-00-work-product-register.md)).

FTTIs for 7 of 9 safety goals, computed from proposed margins and the range of faults the candidate vehicles can produce; the other 2 are met by limits, not by time. The shortest is SG-08 at 357 ms in the worst case. 10 time-critical mechanisms get 40% of their binding FTTI to detect a fault and 40% to react. No value is measured yet.

<!-- END GENERATED: timing-summary -->

**Nothing here is measured, and nothing is final.** The FTTIs are computed from two kinds of input. The *margins* (how far the vehicle may deviate before a goal is at risk) are proposals, to be set with the envelope parameters in [WP-S-02](WP-S-02-technical-safety-requirements.md). The *fault ranges* (how hard a fault can accelerate or steer the vehicle) depend on the vehicle, which is chosen at G2 (DQ-008); until then they span the candidates, and every budget is a range. Once a value is set it is not loosened to make a finding go away.

The model is kept in `timing/timing.yaml`; `timing/timing.py` computes the FTTIs and budgets and writes the tables and Figure 2-1.

## 2. FTTI per safety goal

A goal of kind *deviation* is violated when a fault's acceleration has moved the vehicle by the margin: x = a t² / 2, so FTTI = √(2x / a). A goal of kind *drift* is violated when the vehicle has moved by the margin at a given speed: FTTI = x / v. A *derived* goal takes the shortest FTTI of the goals it depends on. A goal of kind *limit* is met by a limit on a command rather than by detecting a fault in time.

**Remote driving is the tightest.** SG-08 allows a remote command only as old as the time the vehicle needs to move by its drift margin at remote driving speed. At 20 km/h that would have been under 200 ms for the whole path, which a cellular link is unlikely to hold. The lead limited remote driving to walking pace, up to 10 km/h (DQ-032 A, 2026-10-23), which doubles the time; the video delay to the operator still counts against it, and the measurement of §6 must show the link holds it in its worst hours.

## 4. Detection and reaction times of each mechanism

Each time-critical mechanism is bound by the shortest FTTI of the goals it serves. Of that FTTI, a share goes to detecting the fault and a share to reacting (reaching the safe state); the rest is kept in reserve for what measurement will find. The shares are a proposal of this work product, not a decision of the lead.

## 5. Fallback and minimal risk manoeuvre timing

The rated manoeuvres start when their mechanism detects the fault; Autoware's own manoeuvres are QM and not budgeted here. Stop times are those computed in [WP-C-12 §6](../02-concept/WP-C-12-fallback-and-minimal-risk-concept.md#6-fallback-timing-input-to-wp-s-04) from the top of the speed range.

The stop of MRM-4 runs at the firm level of FSR-03.02, raised to emergency braking only when FSR-02.02 requires it; that level, and the vehicle's own stop of MRM-5, are set in WP-S-02 with the vehicle supplier. Detection takes tenths of a second and a stop from the top of the speed range takes seconds, so the stop, not the detection, dominates every fallback; the detection budgets matter because they decide where the stop begins.

## 6. Measurement method

Every budget is confirmed by measurement on the integrated system, not by analysis alone:

- **Chain latency** (§3): time-stamped injection of a target at each sensor, measured to brake pressure at the wheel, on the HIL bench and on the vehicle (WP-S-08).
- **Detection times** (§4): fault injection at each mechanism's input, with the time from injection to the mechanism's flag recorded across many runs; the budget holds at the worst observed value plus the reserve.
- **Reaction times**: from the flag to the vehicle state the safe state requires (deceleration reached, propulsion off, steering held), measured on the vehicle.
- **Remote command age** (SG-08): end-to-end from the operator's input to the monitor's acceptance, over the cellular links of the geofence, including their worst hours.

Results are recorded as evidence in the safety case; a budget that a measurement exceeds is a finding against the design, never a reason to change the FTTI.

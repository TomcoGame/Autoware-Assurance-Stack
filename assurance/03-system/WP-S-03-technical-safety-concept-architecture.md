## 1. Purpose

The technical safety concept and the system architecture that realises it. It takes the elements and functional safety requirements of the [functional safety concept (WP-C-04)](../02-concept/WP-C-04-functional-safety-concept.md), refines the elements into system components, and places on them the safety mechanisms that implement every rated requirement. The technical safety requirements themselves are written from these mechanisms in [WP-S-02](WP-S-02-technical-safety-requirements.md), and their timing in [WP-S-04](WP-S-04-timing-ftti-budget.md).

Needed at gate G2 ([WP-M-00](../01-management/WP-M-00-work-product-register.md)).

<!-- BEGIN GENERATED: tsc-summary (assurance/03-system/tsc/tsc.py) -->

11 system components, 7 of them at ASIL D, carry 19 safety mechanisms that implement all 34 rated functional safety requirements, each on a component of the element the FSR is allocated to. 4 redundancies, each with its diversity and the triggering conditions they share.

<!-- END GENERATED: tsc-summary -->

The concept is kept in `tsc/tsc.yaml`; `tsc/tsc.py` checks it and writes the tables and Figure 2-1. It refuses a rated FSR with no mechanism, a mechanism placed on a component of another element than its FSR's, and a component whose derived ASIL exceeds what the functional safety concept derives for its element.

## 2. Architecture overview (Autoware stack, safety monitor, drive-by-wire)

*Figure 2-1. System architecture with the derived ASIL of each component and the safety mechanisms it hosts. Generated by `tsc/tsc.py` from `tsc/tsc.yaml`.*

The architecture is the doer/checker of DQ-002 A: Autoware drives, and every command it sends reaches the vehicle only through the safety monitor, which bounds it with the safety channel's own perception (DQ-018 A). The only other path into the vehicle's motion is the vehicle's own stop when the monitor falls silent (MRM-5).

The same architecture is available as a machine-readable model for SysML v2 tools, [`tsc/architecture.sysml`](tsc/architecture.sysml): the components as parts with their element and derived ASIL, the links and the redundancies as connections, each safety mechanism as an action allocated to its host with dependencies on the components it uses and the reactions it starts, and the FSRs each mechanism satisfies. `tsc/sysml.py` writes it from `tsc/tsc.yaml` alone, so it holds no data of its own, and refuses a file that does not read back to the same architecture. The file was checked once with the SysML v2 Pilot Implementation 0.62.0 (2026-09 release, `jupyter-sysml-kernel` from conda-forge) against its standard library, with no errors or warnings; CI runs only the read-back, because the Pilot is a Java tool outside the standard library and PyYAML (OI-7).

**The monitor carries most of the weight.** Eight of the mechanisms run on the safety monitor controller. They are deliberately simple: rules over positions, speeds and accelerations, which can be specified completely, verified formally (SM-01, SM-02) and fault-injected. The hard problem, seeing the world, sits in SM-09 on the safety channel processor, and its performance is a SOTIF question as much as a functional safety one (WP-C-06 FI-08).

**The cybersecurity check runs in the monitor.** SM-05 verifies the signature of every remote command (CSR-01). That code is ASIL D software with a timing budget in WP-S-04, as WP-C-10 §4.3 noted.

## 4. Redundancy and diversity

<!-- BEGIN GENERATED: tsc-redundancy (assurance/03-system/tsc/tsc.py) -->

| ID | Between | What | Diversity | Common triggering conditions | Waits on |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RD-01 | CMP-01, CMP-03 | Autoware's perception and the safety channel's | Different sensors (DQ-018 A), learned classification against geometry, different processors and software | TC-02, TC-03, TC-04, TC-15, TC-24 | — |

| RD-03 | CMP-02, CMP-06 | The monitor's stop and the vehicle's own stop when the monitor falls silent | Different controllers and suppliers; the vehicle's stop needs only the absence of the monitor's commands | TC-25 | — |
| RD-04 | CMP-06 | Primary and secondary brake paths | As the vehicle supplier designs them (AOU-01) | TC-25 | — |

<!-- END GENERATED: tsc-redundancy -->

Each redundancy lists the triggering conditions of WP-C-06 that affect both of its sides; those are the starting points of the dependent failure analysis ([WP-A-03](../08-analyses/WP-A-03-dependent-failure-analysis.md)). The lead decided (DQ-031 A, 2026-10-23) that the safety channel's ASIL D detection is decomposed into LiDAR and radar, each developed to ASIL B(D), and a road user seen by either sensor counts. The goal stays ASIL D; the decomposition holds only if RD-02 shows the two sensor chains independent, so the dependent failure analysis must treat spray and dirt (TC-03, TC-24) as common causes, and the rationale goes into [WP-A-01](../08-analyses/WP-A-01-asil-decomposition.md). The generator refuses a decomposition without an answered decision, a partner decomposed the other way, and a redundancy entry between the two.

**A cost of detecting on either sensor.** Counting a road user seen by either sensor is what makes the decomposition work for SG-02, but every false detection of either sensor can now make the monitor brake. FSR-03.01 (false detections rare enough for SG-03) must therefore be met by each sensor chain on its own, which WP-S-02 states as a technical safety requirement per sensor. The decomposition does not split that requirement, so the lead decided (DQ-035 A, 2026-10-24) that each sensor is developed to ASIL C for false detections as well as to ASIL B(D) for detection; §2 shows both.

## 5. AI architecture measures

The AI systems of [WP-C-11](../02-concept/WP-C-11-ai-system-definition-and-safety-requirements.md) all run on the Autoware computer (CMP-01, QM). The architecture measures ISO/PAS 8800 asks for are:

- **Bounding by a non-AI channel.** SM-01, SM-02 and SM-09 bound the effect of any AI error with rules and geometry; no AI output reaches the vehicle without passing them (AISR-13).
- **No AI on a rated component.** The generator of WP-C-11 refuses an AI system on a rated element; SM-09 uses no learned classification.
- **Monitoring in operation.** Out-of-distribution inputs and performance drops are monitored and fed back (AISR-14, WP-O-06).
